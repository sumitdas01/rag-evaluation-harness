# THE DIGITAL PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION ACT, 2023 (INDIA)
**Act No. 22 of 2023**
*Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative Department), Government of India*

---

## CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY AND DEFINITIONS

### Section 2: Definitions
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires:
- **(a) "Board"** means the Data Protection Board of India established by the Central Government under Section 18.
- **(i) "Data Fiduciary"** means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data.
- **(j) "Data Principal"** means the individual to whom the personal data relates and where such individual is—
  - (i) a child, includes the parents or lawful guardian of such a child;
  - (ii) a person with disability, includes her lawful guardian, acting on her behalf.
- **(k) "Data Processor"** means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
- **(q) "Personal Data"** means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data.
- **(r) "Personal Data Breach"** means any unauthorized processing of personal data or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction of or loss of access to personal data, that compromises the confidentiality, integrity or availability of personal data.
- **(y) "Significant Data Fiduciary"** means any Data Fiduciary or class of Data Fiduciaries as may be notified by the Central Government under Section 10 based on factors such as volume of data, risk of harm, potential impact on sovereignty, and state security.

## CHAPTER II: OBLIGATIONS OF DATA FIDUCIARY

### Section 4: Grounds for Processing Personal Data
(1) A person may process the personal data of an individual only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and for a lawful purpose—
  - (a) for which the Data Principal has given her consent; or
  - (b) for certain legitimate uses as specified in Section 7.

### Section 5: Notice Requirements
(1) Every request for consent under Section 6 shall be preceded or accompanied by a notice given by the Data Fiduciary to the Data Principal informing her:
  - (a) the personal data sought to be collected and the purpose for which it will be processed;
  - (b) the manner in which she may exercise her rights under Section 12 and Section 13; and
  - (c) the manner in which she may make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India.
(2) The Data Fiduciary shall give the Data Principal the option to access the notice in English or any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India.

### Section 6: Consent
(1) Consent given by the Data Principal shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action.
(2) The Data Principal shall have the right to withdraw her consent at any time with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given.
(3) The withdrawal of consent shall not affect the legality of processing of personal data based on consent before its withdrawal.

### Section 7: Certain Legitimate Uses
A Data Fiduciary may process personal data of a Data Principal for certain legitimate uses, including:
  - (a) where the Data Principal has voluntarily provided her personal data for a specified purpose and has not indicated disapproval;
  - (b) for the State and its instrumentalities to provide subsidies, benefits, services, certificates, or licenses;
  - (c) for compliance with any judgment, decree, or order issued under law;
  - (d) for responding to a medical emergency involving a threat to the life or health of the Data Principal;
  - (e) for taking measures to provide medical treatment or health services during an epidemic or public health crisis;
  - (f) for taking measures to undertake assistance or relief during any disaster or breakdown of public order;
  - (g) for the purposes of employment or related to safeguarding an employer from loss or liability.

### Section 8: General Obligations of Data Fiduciary
(1) A Data Fiduciary shall, irrespective of any agreement to the contrary, be responsible for complying with the provisions of this Act in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor.
(2) A Data Fiduciary may engage, appoint, use, or involve a Data Processor only under a valid contract.
(3) The Data Fiduciary shall ensure accuracy, completeness, and consistency of personal data if it is used to make a decision that affects the Data Principal.
(4) A Data Fiduciary shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure effective observance of this Act.
(5) A Data Fiduciary shall protect personal data in its possession or under its control by taking reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach.
(6) In the event of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary shall give the Board and each affected Data Principal intimation of such breach in such form and manner as prescribed.
(7) A Data Fiduciary must erase personal data upon withdrawal of consent or as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served, unless retention is necessary for legal compliance.

### Section 9: Processing of Personal Data of Children
(1) A Data Fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child or person with disability, obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian.
(2) A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake such processing of personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child.
(3) A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioral monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children.
(4) "Child" means an individual who has not completed eighteen years of age.

### Section 10: Additional Obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary
A Significant Data Fiduciary must:
  - (a) appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) based in India who shall represent the Significant Data Fiduciary and report directly to the Board of Directors or governing body;
  - (b) appoint an independent data auditor to evaluate compliance; and
  - (c) undertake Periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA), audit of processes, and other measures as prescribed.

## CHAPTER III: RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF DATA PRINCIPAL

### Section 11: Right to Access Information
The Data Principal shall have the right to obtain from the Data Fiduciary:
  - (a) a summary of personal data being processed and the processing activities undertaken;
  - (b) the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom the personal data has been shared;
  - (c) any other information related to personal data and its processing as prescribed.

### Section 12: Right to Correction and Erasure
A Data Principal shall have the right to correction, completion, updating, and erasure of her personal data for the processing of which she has previously given consent.

### Section 13: Right of Grievance Redressal
A Data Principal shall have the right to readily available means of grievance redressal provided by a Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager in respect of any act or omission regarding performance of obligations. The Data Principal must exhaust internal grievance mechanisms before approaching the Board.

### Section 14: Right to Nominate
A Data Principal shall have the right to nominate any other individual who shall, in the event of death or incapacity of the Data Principal, exercise her rights under this Act.

### Section 15: Duties of Data Principal
A Data Principal shall comply with all provisions of applicable laws, not impersonate another person, not register false or frivolous grievances, and furnish only verifiably authentic information when applying for documents or services.

---

## CHAPTER IV: SPECIAL PROVISIONS & EXEMPTIONS

### Section 16: Cross-Border Transfer of Personal Data
The Central Government may, by notification, restrict the transfer of personal data by a Data Fiduciary for processing to such country or territory outside India as may be specified (the "blacklisting" or negative-list approach). Where any other law provides for a higher degree of protection or restriction on transfer outside India, that law shall prevail.

### Section 17: Exemptions
(1) Provisions of Chapter II (Obligations), Chapter III (Rights and Duties), and Section 16 shall not apply where:
  - (a) processing is necessary for enforcing any legal right or claim;
  - (b) processing is necessary for the prevention, detection, investigation, or prosecution of any offense;
  - (c) processing by court, tribunal, or quasi-judicial body in India;
  - (d) personal data of non-residents processed in India under contract with foreign entities.
(2) The Central Government may exempt state instrumentalities in the interests of sovereignty, integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign states, or maintenance of public order.
(3) Startups and certain classes of Data Fiduciaries may be exempted from provisions of Section 5 (Notice), Section 8(3), Section 8(7) (Erasure), and Section 10 (Significant Data Fiduciaries).

## CHAPTER V & THE SCHEDULE: PENALTIES

### Section 33 & Schedule: Monetary Penalties
If the Data Protection Board of India determines non-compliance after inquiry, it may impose financial penalties as specified in the Schedule:
1. **Breach in observing obligation of reasonable security safeguards** to prevent personal data breach under Section 8(5): Up to **₹250 crore** (Two hundred and fifty crore rupees).
2. **Failure to notify the Board and affected Data Principals of personal data breach** under Section 8(6): Up to **₹200 crore** (Two hundred crore rupees).
3. **Breach in observance of additional obligations in relation to children** under Section 9: Up to **₹200 crore** (Two hundred crore rupees).
4. **Breach in observance of additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary** under Section 10: Up to **₹150 crore** (One hundred and fifty crore rupees).
5. **Breach in observance of duties by Data Principal** under Section 15: Up to **₹10,000** (Ten thousand rupees).
6. **General breach of any other provision of this Act or rules**: Up to **₹50 crore** (Fifty crore rupees).
